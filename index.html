Genome-informed STR marker discovery for wildlife forensics

Why does it matter?
Illegal wildlife trade threatens biodiversity and creates significant challenges for law enforcement and wildlife forensic laboratories. Genetic evidence can help establish the identity and origin of seized animals and animal-derived materials, but suitable genetic markers are not available for every taxon. STR-WISE addresses this gap by systematically screening available genome data for variable tetranucleotide STRs. Its genus-oriented approach aims to identify candidate markers that can be applied across multiple species within a genus, helping to guide the development of broadly useful genetic marker panels.

How does STR-WISE work?
A bioinformatic pipeline identifies tetranucleotide STRs in the available genomes of protected animals. Candidate STR loci are compared across available genomes to assess their variability and suitability for further marker development. The pipeline identifies promising STR candidates with the potential to work across a genus, providing a starting point for developing genetic marker panels for wildlife forensic applications.

Applications

Forensic and conservation applications of the STR-WISE

  • Illegal Wildlife Trade

    Supports the detection of illegal trade involving wildlife and related products and other derivatives.

  • Human Crime Investigations

    Assists criminal investigations where wildlife evidence is present, such as horse hair on suspects or traces from crime scenes involving stables.

  • Pedigree Verification

    Provides genetic verification of pedigree records, supporting the authentication of identities in breeding and trade.

  • Theft

    Supports the forensic identification of wildlife in theft cases, including disputes over ownership and animal identity.

  • Illegal Wildlife Trade

    Supports the detection of illegal trade involving wild equids and related products and other derivatives.

  • Genetic Diversity

    Enables the assessment of genetic diversity in wildlife populations, supporting conservation research and wildlife management strategies.
